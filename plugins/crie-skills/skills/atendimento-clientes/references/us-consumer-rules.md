# US consumer rules for customer service (plain language, not legal advice)

Federal rules apply everywhere; states add more. When the stakes are high (injury, large amount, lawyer letter,
Attorney General/BBB complaint), tell the owner to talk to an attorney before replying.

## Returns and refunds
- **No general federal right to return or cancel** a purchase. The business sets its policy — but must honor what it
  advertised and posted, and apply it consistently.
- **State posting rules** (examples — check the business's state):
  - California (Civ. Code §1723): a store that doesn't give a full cash refund, exchange or credit within 7 days
    must post its policy; otherwise customers can return within 30 days.
  - New York (Gen. Bus. Law §218-a): the refund policy must be posted; without it, returns of unused goods are
    allowed within 30 days for cash or credit refund.
  - Florida (Stat. §501.142): a retailer with a no-refund policy must post it; without the notice, unused goods can
    be returned within 7 days of purchase for a refund (in the original carton, if one was furnished).
- Refund to the original payment method; say how many days the card company takes to post it.
- Custom or perishable goods (cakes, personalized items, special orders): say "final sale" / cancellation cutoff
  clearly **before** payment (invoice, website, order confirmation).

## Late orders (FTC Mail, Internet, or Telephone Order Merchandise Rule)
- Have a reasonable basis to ship within the stated time; if no time is stated, within 30 days.
- If you can't, notify the customer before the deadline with a new date and the option to cancel for a prompt
  refund (card: within 1 billing cycle; other methods: within 7 business days). Silence after the first delay counts
  as consent only for a delay of up to 30 days.
- Services and local same-day orders aren't covered by this rule, but the same honesty applies (FTC Act §5).

## Defects and warranties
- **Implied warranty of merchantability** (UCC §2-314): goods sold by a merchant must be fit for their ordinary
  purpose. It can be disclaimed ("as is") in most states, but not for consumer goods in some (e.g., Massachusetts,
  Maryland, Vermont, West Virginia, Maine, DC, Kansas, Mississippi).
- **Written warranties** (Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act): if you give one, state it clearly (full vs. limited) and
  honor it. A written warranty means you can't disclaim implied warranties.
- Services: perform with reasonable skill ("workmanlike"); offer to redo or refund the affected part.
- Food: unsafe or spoiled food → replace or refund right away; never "repair". Possible illness or allergic
  reaction → empathy, collect facts, no admission of liability by message, notify the insurer (general/product
  liability), keep samples and records, cooperate with the local health department. Allergy questions: answer only
  with what the recipe and label actually say ("made in a kitchen that also handles nuts").

## Cancellations
- **FTC Cooling-Off Rule** (16 CFR Part 429): sales of $25+ at the buyer's home, or $130+ at a temporary location
  (fair, hotel room, convention), can be cancelled within 3 business days; give the customer the written notice and
  two cancellation forms; refund within 10 business days of cancellation. Exceptions include real estate, insurance,
  securities, emergency home repairs the buyer requested, and sales completed entirely by phone or online. Many
  states have similar home-solicitation laws (e.g., Massachusetts c. 93 §48) — sometimes broader.
- **Subscriptions, memberships, recurring service plans**: the FTC's 2024 "click-to-cancel" amendments to the
  Negative Option Rule were vacated by the Eighth Circuit on 07/08/2025; the FTC restarted the rulemaking in 2026
  (status may change — check). Still in force: **ROSCA** (clear terms before billing, express informed consent, a
  simple way to cancel) and **state automatic-renewal laws** (e.g., California — online cancellation, renewal
  reminders, 2025 amendments; New York, Massachusetts and others). Good practice anywhere: cancel the same way they
  signed up, confirm in writing, no surprise charges.
- Deposits: refundable or not per the written terms the customer agreed to; unclear terms usually favor the customer.

## Chargebacks (Fair Credit Billing Act + card network rules)
- The cardholder disputes with their bank; the business answers through the processor (Square, Stripe, Clover,
  Toast, PayPal) by the deadline shown — often 7–20 days.
- Evidence: invoice/receipt, signed estimate or contract, policy shown at checkout, delivery confirmation or photos,
  messages showing the customer received/approved, AVS/CVV match, IP for online orders.
- Don't refund outside the dispute (risk of paying twice). If the customer is right, accept the dispute.
- Friendly fraud: track repeat disputers; ask for signatures/photos on delivery for high-value orders.

## Reviews (FTC Rule on Consumer Reviews and Testimonials, 16 CFR Part 465, effective 10/21/2024)
- Banned: fake/AI-generated reviews, buying reviews, incentives conditioned on positive sentiment, undisclosed
  insider reviews, suppressing negative reviews with threats or false claims, fake "independent" review sites.
- Google prohibits incentives for any review and selective solicitation (gating); Yelp prohibits asking for reviews.
- Responding: polite, brief, no personal or health information, take it offline.

## Collecting what customers owe
- The **FDCPA** covers third-party collectors, not usually a business collecting its own invoices — but state law
  may: California Rosenthal Act, Florida Consumer Collection Practices Act (Stat. §559.72), Texas Finance Code ch.
  392, Massachusetts 940 CMR 7.00 (limits on contact frequency, e.g., no more than 2 calls in 7 days per debt), and
  others.
- Always: truthful amounts, no threats of arrest/jail or actions you won't take, no contact with employers, family
  or social media about the debt, no calls/texts before 8am or after 9pm, stop contacting a channel if asked, offer
  a payment plan, send a written statement on request. Small claims court is the normal next step for small amounts.
- Texts about a customer's own account are transactional, but the customer can still revoke consent to texts (TCPA).

## Texting and messaging (TCPA, carrier rules, platform rules)
- Replies to a customer who wrote first are fine. **Marketing texts** need prior express written consent; include
  business name and "Reply STOP to opt out"; honor opt-outs promptly (within 10 business days at most). Quiet hours
  8am–9pm recipient's time; stricter states (e.g., Florida 8am–8pm, max 3 per 24h).
- WhatsApp Business: outside the 24-hour window after the customer's last message, only approved templates (API);
  marketing templates need opt-in. Instagram/Facebook: automated replies within 24h of the customer's message.
- Email: CAN-SPAM for marketing (address, unsubscribe within 10 business days).

## Privacy and data
- No full card numbers, SSNs or passwords in chat. PCI-DSS: card data only through the processor.
- Health businesses (clinics, med spas, dentists, therapists): messages with patients are PHI under HIPAA; don't
  confirm someone is a patient in public replies. Consumer AI plans aren't covered by a BAA: use de-identified text
  when asking Claude to draft replies.
- Data breach: every state has a notification law; tell the owner to call an attorney.

## Regulated businesses
- **Insurance**: only licensed agents discuss coverage; binding, changes and cancellations follow the carrier's
  written process. State unfair-trade-practice laws ban misrepresenting coverage. Financial institutions
  (including insurance agencies) handle nonpublic personal information under **GLBA** (privacy notice, Safeguards
  Rule): collect SSNs, medical and bank details only by phone, in person or through encrypted portals.
- **Medicare (CMS)**: no unsolicited contact by text, call, voicemail or door-to-door; a Scope of Appointment
  before discussing plans (the 48-hour waiting period was removed for marketing from 10/01/2026, CY2027 rule); CMS disclaimer; call recording requirements for
  marketing calls. Confirm current CMS rules each year.
- **Advisers and brokers**: SEC and FINRA recordkeeping rules require business messages to be preserved; firms
  were fined heavily (2021–2025) for staff using WhatsApp, iMessage and personal texts. Use the firm's approved,
  archived tools. No guarantees (FINRA 2210, SEC Marketing Rule).
- **Lenders**: ECOA/Reg B — treat applicants consistently; TILA/Reg Z — rates as APR with disclosures; adverse
  action notices in writing. No "pre-approved" claims you can't honor.
- **Credit repair (CROA, 15 U.S.C. 1679)**: no payment before services are fully performed, written contract,
  3-business-day cancellation right, required disclosure statement; many states also require registration/bond.
- **Contractors**: written contracts (required for home improvement in many states, e.g., Massachusetts for jobs
  over $1,000 by a registered HIC, with a 3-day cancellation right for home solicitation sales); change orders in
  writing before extra work; permits are the contractor's responsibility unless the contract says otherwise;
  mechanics' lien rights depend on preliminary notices and deadlines that vary by state (e.g., Florida Notice to
  Owner, California preliminary notice) — check with an attorney; warranty callbacks per the written warranty.

## Model return/refund policy (adapt; post it on the website, receipts and at the counter)

**EN** — *Refunds & Returns.* Unused items in original condition can be returned within [X] days with a receipt for
a refund to the original payment method. Custom orders, cakes and perishable items are final sale once production
starts ([X] hours/days before pickup); if anything is wrong with your order, contact us within [24 hours] with a
photo and we'll replace it or refund you. Services: if you're not satisfied, tell us within [48 hours] and we'll
come back to fix the areas at no charge. Deposits are [refundable until X / non-refundable] as stated on your
invoice. Recurring plans can be cancelled anytime by [text/email/online] with [X] days' notice before the next visit.

**PT** — *Trocas e reembolsos.* Itens sem uso e na embalagem original podem ser devolvidos em até [X] dias com o
recibo, com reembolso no mesmo meio de pagamento. Encomendas personalizadas, bolos e perecíveis não têm devolução
depois que a produção começa ([X] horas/dias antes da retirada); se houver algum problema com o pedido, fale com a
gente em até [24 horas] com uma foto e trocamos ou devolvemos o valor. Serviços: se não ficar satisfeito, avise em
até [48 horas] e voltamos para refazer os pontos sem custo. O sinal é [reembolsável até X / não reembolsável],
conforme a fatura. Planos recorrentes podem ser cancelados a qualquer momento por [SMS/e-mail/online] com [X] dias
de antecedência da próxima visita.
