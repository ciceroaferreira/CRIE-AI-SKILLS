## How the business works
- Revenue: monthly tuition or memberships, lesson packages, per-session fees, summer camps and holiday programs, registration and material fees, online courses.
- Costs: instructors (employees or 1099 contractors), rent, materials, insurance, scheduling/payment software (e.g., Brightwheel, Procare, My Music Staff, Square), marketing.
- Seasonality: back to school (August/September), January restart, summer camps; churn around holidays and summer.
- Winners: low churn, visible progress for students and parents, waitlists, referrals from parents, and reliable instructors. Many serve Brazilian families (Portuguese heritage language classes, ESL for adults).

## Key metrics
- Active students and monthly churn
- Occupancy per class/time slot and waitlist size
- Revenue per student and per instructor hour
- Trial-to-enrollment conversion
- Late or missed payments
- Parent satisfaction (NPS/reviews) and student progress milestones

## Regulation, confidentiality and risks
- Children's data (names, ages, schools, photos, health/allergy info, custody arrangements) → `elevated` level at minimum.
- COPPA: online services directed to children under 13 need verifiable parental consent before collecting personal information.
- FERPA applies to education records at schools receiving federal funds; private tutoring centers are usually governed by contracts and state law instead, but treat student records as confidential anyway. Schools you partner with may require FERPA-compliant handling.
- Background checks for staff and volunteers working with minors (state rules vary; childcare usually requires fingerprint checks); staff are often mandated reporters of suspected abuse.
- State childcare licensing for daycare and many after-school programs (ratios, space, training, inspections).
- Photo/video consent from parents before posting; never tag children or show location details.
- Payment terms and refund/withdrawal policies in writing; marketing texts need consent (TCPA).
