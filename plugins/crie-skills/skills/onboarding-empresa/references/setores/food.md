## How the business works
- Revenue: dine-in/counter, takeout, delivery apps (DoorDash, Uber Eats, Grubhub), own online ordering, catering and events, custom orders (cakes), wholesale to cafés and markets, farmers markets.
- Costs: food cost (typically 25–35% of price), labor (25–35%), rent/commissary, packaging, card processing (~2.6–3.5%), delivery app commissions (~15–30% depending on plan — confirm your contract), waste.
- Food trucks: commissary fees, permits per city/county, events and catering are often the most profitable days.
- Winners: costed recipes for every item, a lean and profitable menu (menu engineering), waste control, stable staff, and great reviews.

## Key metrics
- Food cost % and margin per item (recipe costing)
- Prime cost (food + labor) as % of sales (target often ≤ 60–65%)
- Average check and covers/orders per day
- Margin per channel (in-store vs. apps vs. own online ordering vs. catering)
- Waste ($ or %) and inventory variance
- Rating on Google/Yelp/apps and staff turnover

## Regulation, confidentiality and risks
- Local health department permits and inspections; the FDA Food Code is the model, but each state/county adopts its own version. Certified Food Protection Manager (e.g., ServSafe) and food handler cards where required; temperature logs; HACCP plans for special processes.
- Allergens: 9 major allergens (milk, eggs, fish, shellfish, tree nuts, peanuts, wheat, soybeans and sesame, added by the FASTER Act) must be labeled on packaged foods (FALCPA); train staff on cross-contact and disclose on menus where local rules require.
- Cottage food laws are state-specific: which foods are allowed, labeling ("Made in a cottage food operation…"), where you can sell, and revenue caps in some states (e.g., Florida allows cottage food sales up to $250,000 a year, s. 500.80). Selling wholesale or shipping may require a licensed kitchen.
- Food trucks: commissary agreement, mobile vendor permits, fire inspection, parking/zoning rules by city.
- Sales tax usually applies to prepared food (rules vary by state); delivery apps collect it as marketplace facilitators in many states.
- Labor: FLSA minimum wage and tip credit rules, tip pooling, state minimum wages; employee vs. 1099.
- Customer data from online orders → `standard` (or `elevated` with a large customer list).
