## How the business works
- Revenue: sales commissions split between brokerage and agent (always negotiable), buyer-agent compensation agreed in writing, property management fees (often 8–12% of rent plus leasing fees — verify your market), short-term rental management (15–30% of bookings).
- Costs: brokerage splits and fees, MLS and association dues, marketing, licensing and E&O insurance, vehicle.
- Pipeline business: leads → appointments → signed agreements → under contract → closed; long cycles and seasonality (spring/summer peaks).
- Winners: consistent follow-up, a database/CRM they actually use, local market expertise, strong reviews and referrals; many agents serve the Brazilian community in Portuguese.

## Key metrics
- Leads per source and lead-to-appointment conversion
- Signed buyer/listing agreements per month
- Closed transactions and gross commission income (GCI)
- Average days on market and list-to-sale price ratio
- Pipeline value (under contract) and fall-through rate
- Property management: occupancy, rent collected on time, maintenance response time

## Regulation, confidentiality and risks
- State real estate license law and commission rules; brokers must supervise agents; advertising must show the brokerage name.
- Fair Housing Act: no discriminatory language in listings or ads about protected classes (race, color, religion, sex incl. sexual orientation and gender identity, national origin, familial status, disability; many states add more, e.g., source of income). Describe the property, not who should live there ("great for young couples" is a problem). Claude checks every listing for this.
- 2024 NAR settlement changes: written buyer agreements before touring, compensation not on the MLS — verify current rules with your broker and MLS.
- RESPA (no kickbacks for settlement service referrals), MLS rules, Do Not Call and TCPA for prospecting calls/texts, CAN-SPAM.
- Wire fraud: never send wiring instructions by email; always confirm by phone with a known number.
- Property management: security deposit rules vary by state (limits, separate account, interest, return deadlines); tenant screening uses consumer reports under the FCRA (adverse action notices); landlord-tenant law and eviction procedures are state-specific.
- Data: client finances, pre-approval letters, IDs, tenant applications with SSNs → `elevated` level.
