## How the business works
- Revenue: monthly retainers and managed services (per user/device for MSPs), projects (websites, apps), SaaS subscriptions (MRR), hourly support, ad management fees (% of ad spend or flat).
- Costs: people (the main cost), software licenses and cloud, subcontractors/freelancers, sales.
- Risks: scope creep, dependence on 1–3 big clients, churn, security incidents with client systems.
- Winners: recurring revenue, productized offers with clear scope, documented processes, strong security hygiene and case studies that sell.

## Key metrics
- Monthly recurring revenue (MRR) and net revenue retention
- Client churn and revenue concentration (% from top 3 clients)
- Gross margin per client/project (real hours vs. sold)
- Utilization (billable hours) and backlog
- SaaS: CAC, LTV, LTV/CAC, trial-to-paid conversion
- Support: response and resolution time, SLA met

## Regulation, confidentiality and risks
- Client data, credentials and source code under NDA and contracts (MSA + SOW, data processing addendum) → `elevated` level, or `regulated` if you serve healthcare (HIPAA BAA), financial or legal clients.
- State privacy laws (CCPA/CPRA in California and a growing list of others) if you process consumers' personal data, plus FTC Act §5 (no unfair/deceptive practices, keep security promises) and state breach notification laws.
- Enterprise clients often expect SOC 2 reports, security questionnaires, cyber liability insurance and MFA everywhere.
- Intellectual property: written IP assignment from employees and contractors (work for hire), license terms for deliverables, open-source licenses.
- Credentials: use a password manager and per-client access; never put client passwords, API keys or customer data into prompts, tickets or shared docs.
- Agencies: FTC Endorsement Guides and fake reviews rule for client campaigns; TCPA and CAN-SPAM for messaging campaigns.
