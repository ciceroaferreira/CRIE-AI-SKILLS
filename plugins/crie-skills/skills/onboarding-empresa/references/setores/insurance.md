## How the business works
- Revenue: first-year commissions plus renewals (trails) paid by carriers; Medicare Advantage/Part D and ACA plans pay per enrollment and renewal; annuities pay a % of premium. Some agencies share commissions with downline agents (hierarchy/IMO/FMO).
- Costs: leads (purchased or ads), CRM/quoting tools, E&O insurance, licensing and CE, staff for service.
- Seasonality: Medicare Annual Enrollment Period (AEP, Oct 15–Dec 7) and ACA Open Enrollment (2027 coverage: Nov 1, 2026–Jan 15, 2027 on HealthCare.gov, which Florida uses; enroll by Dec 15 for Jan 1 coverage; state exchanges set their own dates — verify each year).
- Winners: consistent follow-up, documented needs analysis, high persistency (few lapses), referrals, clean compliance files, bilingual service for the community.

## Key metrics
- Applications submitted vs. placed (issued and paid)
- Persistency / lapse rate at 13 months
- Lead cost and cost per placed policy, by source
- Contact-to-appointment and appointment-to-application rates
- Renewal commission base (book of business)
- Not-taken-out and declined rates; time from application to issue
- CE hours completed vs. due; complaints

## Regulation, confidentiality and risks
- Licensing: state producer license by line (life, accident & health) in the resident state; non-resident licenses for each other state where you sell; carrier appointments before soliciting (rules vary by state). CE every renewal cycle, incl. ethics and annuity training where required.
- Annuities: suitability / best-interest standard under NAIC Suitability in Annuity Transactions Model Regulation (#275, 2020 best-interest revision adopted in 49 jurisdictions per NAIC, Aug 2025 — verify yours): gather the consumer profile, document the basis for the recommendation, disclose material conflicts.
- Replacements: state replacement regulations require replacement forms/notices and comparison; never recommend replacing a policy without documented consumer benefit.
- Illustrations: use only insurer-approved illustrations from the carrier's software. **Never invent or estimate policy values, cash values, dividends, rates or guarantees.** Claude can explain an illustration the agent provides, not create one.
- Advertising: state insurance department rules (based on NAIC advertising models); no misleading comparisons, no "free" or "guaranteed" claims beyond the contract; carrier approval often required for ads.
- Anti-rebating: most states prohibit giving anything of value to induce a sale beyond limits set by law (some states allow small gifts or value-added services — verify).
- Medicare (CMS marketing rules): Scope of Appointment documented before a personal marketing appointment (the 48-hour wait was removed by the CY2027 rule for marketing from Oct 1, 2026), call recording rules, required disclaimers, no cold door-knocking or unsolicited texts; carrier certification each year (AHIP + carrier). Verify the current CMS Medicare Communications and Marketing Guidelines.
- Leads: TCPA (prior express written consent for marketing calls/texts with autodialers or prerecorded messages), National Do-Not-Call Registry and state DNC lists, quiet hours in some states.
- Privacy: GLBA privacy notice and FTC Safeguards Rule / state insurance data security laws (NAIC Insurance Data Security Model Law in many states) with a written information security plan. Applicant medical information (health history, prescriptions, MIB) is highly sensitive; HIPAA often applies through carriers and health plans. Consumer Claude plans have no BAA: use de-identified data only.
- E&O insurance is expected (often required by carriers).
- → `regulated`: client SSNs, DOBs, health history, bank info for drafts, policy numbers.
