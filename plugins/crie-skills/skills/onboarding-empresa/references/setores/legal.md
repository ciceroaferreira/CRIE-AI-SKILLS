## How the business works
- Revenue: flat fees (very common in immigration: per petition/application, often in installments), hourly billing against a retainer held in trust, contingency fees (personal injury, typically 33–40%), monthly subscription/general counsel plans for businesses.
- Main cost: attorney and paralegal time; the bottleneck is usually partner review time, intake and deadline control.
- Immigration practices serving the Brazilian community run on volume: consultations, USCIS filings (family petitions, adjustment of status, naturalization, work permits, asylum), removal defense in immigration court (EOIR), and constant client questions about case status, often by WhatsApp.
- Winners: zero missed deadlines, clear flat-fee menus, fast intake and conflicts check, bilingual client communication, and a steady referral network.

## Key metrics
- Missed or near-missed deadlines (goal zero) and RFEs received per 100 filings
- New consultations per month and consultation-to-retained conversion rate
- Average fee per matter and accounts receivable / installment plans past due
- Matters per attorney/paralegal and hours per flat-fee matter (real profitability)
- Time to first response to clients and time from retention to filing
- Trust account reconciled monthly (yes/no) and online review rating

## Regulation, confidentiality and risks
- ABA Model Rule 1.6 (confidentiality), attorney-client privilege and work product: everything about clients and matters is confidential → `regulated` level. State versions of the rules control; state bar AI opinions vary (e.g., CA, FL, NY, NJ, TX have guidance — check yours).
- ABA Formal Opinion 512 (2024) on generative AI: Rule 1.1 competence (incl. cmt. 8, technology), get informed client consent before putting confidential information into self-learning tools, verify every output (citations can be wrong or invented), supervise AI like nonlawyer assistance (Rule 5.3), keep fees reasonable (Rule 1.5) and communicate with clients about AI use when relevant (Rule 1.4).
- Rule 1.5: contingency fee agreements must be in writing; many states require written fee agreements for all matters. IOLTA/trust accounting rules: unearned fees stay in trust; never commingle.
- Attorney advertising (state Rule 7.x): no misleading claims, no guaranteed outcomes, "Attorney Advertising" labels where required, rules on testimonials and solicitation.
- Conflicts checks before taking any matter (opposing parties, co-defendants, family members in immigration cases).
- Immigration: USCIS receipt numbers, A-Numbers, EOIR hearing dates and filing deadlines, RFE/NOID response windows are critical data and deadlines. Warn against "notario" fraud: never present non-lawyer staff or Claude as able to give legal advice. Foreign documents need certified English translations.
- Personal data of opposing parties, witnesses and family members is also confidential.
