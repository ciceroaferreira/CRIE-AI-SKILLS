## How the business works
- Revenue: recurring visits (weekly/biweekly house cleaning, salon regulars), one-off jobs (deep cleaning, move-out, post-construction), packages and memberships, commercial contracts (offices, Airbnb turnovers), per-page/per-word translation.
- Costs: labor (the biggest), supplies, transportation/mileage, insurance and bonding, software (Jobber, Housecall Pro, Square Appointments, Vagaro, Booksy), card processing (~2.6–3.5%).
- Payment methods: card, Zelle, Venmo Business, cash, checks; deposits for new clients.
- Winners: recurring clients on a fixed schedule, clear quotes and policies (cancellation, lockout, pets), reliable teams, and lots of Google reviews.

## Key metrics
- Recurring clients and churn per month
- Average ticket per visit/job and revenue per labor hour
- Labor cost as % of revenue (target often 40–55% in cleaning — verify for your market)
- Quote-to-booking conversion rate
- Rebooking rate (salons) and no-show/late-cancel rate
- Google rating and number of reviews; complaints/re-cleans per 100 jobs

## Regulation, confidentiality and risks
- Workers: employee vs. 1099 contractor classification is a big risk. Massachusetts, New Jersey and California use strict "ABC tests"; misclassification brings back taxes and penalties. Contractors fill a W-9 and get a 1099-NEC (threshold $600 through 2025; raised to $2,000 for payments from 2026 — verify). Confirm with your CPA/attorney.
- Insurance: general liability, workers' comp where required, janitorial bond for cleaning; commercial clients ask for a certificate of insurance (COI).
- Beauty: state cosmetology/esthetics/nail licenses for each professional and the shop; sanitation rules from the state board.
- Home services sold at the customer's home may fall under the FTC Cooling-Off Rule (3 business days to cancel certain sales). Some states license or register home improvement work.
- Sensitive client data: home addresses, door/garage codes, alarm codes, keys, pet and schedule info (when the home is empty) → `elevated` level. Keep access codes out of shared spreadsheets and group chats.
- Marketing texts need prior express written consent (TCPA); reviews must be genuine (FTC rule on fake reviews).
