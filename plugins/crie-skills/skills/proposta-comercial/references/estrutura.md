# Detailed proposal structure (US)

Write the sections in the proposal language (English by default). Portuguese section names are in parentheses for Portuguese proposals.

## Cover (Capa)
- Outcome-oriented title, not service-oriented: "More bookings from Instagram in 90 days" instead of "Social media management proposal".
- Customer, contact, date, proposal number `YYYY-NNN`, valid until.
- Sender: business name (or professional name for freelancers), state, and — only if the user provided them — license/registration number, EIN, insurance carrier. Otherwise `[fill in]`. Never an SSN.

## 1. Understanding of the need (Entendimento da necessidade) — 1 paragraph + 3 bullets
Restate the problem in the customer's words. Show you listened: quote numbers or situations they mentioned. End with the expected outcome ("By the end, you will have…").

## 2. Scope of work (Escopo do trabalho)
Numbered steps (it is a real sequence). For each: what happens and what the customer must provide (access, materials, keys, approvals).

## 3. Deliverables (Entregáveis)
Objective, verifiable list: "12 posts per month with captions", "Lunch for 40 people, delivered and set up by 11:45 am", "2 rounds of revisions".

## 4. Timeline (Cronograma)
| Step | Time | Depends on |
|---|---|---|
| Kickoff | 2 business days | Signed proposal + deposit |

State that timelines start after approval and deposit.

## 5. Investment (Investimento)

### Good / Better / Best (when it makes sense)
| | Good | **Better (recommended)** | Best |
|---|---|---|---|
| Includes | … | … | … |
| Price | $ | $ | $ |

Rule: "Better" solves the core problem; "Good" is the entry point; "Best" anchors the price.

### Price lines
- Line items → **Subtotal** → **Sales tax ([rate]% — [state/county], confirm)** only on taxable items → **Total**.
- If nothing is taxable, omit the line or write "Sales tax: not applicable (confirm with your state)". Never fold tax into the price silently.
- B2B buyers with a resale/exemption certificate: no tax on the covered items; keep the certificate on file.

### Payment terms — one-time job
- Deposit (commonly 25–50%) to book, balance on completion; or milestone billing (e.g., 40% / 30% / 30%) for longer projects. Show each amount in dollars.
- B2B: invoice terms Net 15 or Net 30 from invoice date. Late fee only if stated in the agreement and allowed by state law — let the user set the rate.
- Accepted methods: ACH/bank transfer, check, Zelle, card (Square/Stripe/etc.), PayPal/Venmo Business. Use `[fill in]` for account details; send them only on the invoice, never bank numbers in a public document.
- Card surcharge: only where state law allows (banned in CT, ME and MA; capped at 2% in CO; other states add disclosure rules — verify yours), within network caps (Visa 3%, Mastercard 4%), never on debit/prepaid, disclosed up front. Alternative: a cash/ACH discount.

### Payment terms — recurring service
- Price per visit/period and billing frequency (per visit, every two weeks, monthly in advance), due date and auto-pay option.
- Monthly equivalent when billing monthly: weekly = 52/12 = 4.33 visits/month; biweekly = 26/12 = 2.17 visits/month.
- Annual price review: fixed % or "with 30 days' written notice".
- Minimum term (if any) and what happens if cancelled earlier; cancellation notice (e.g., 14 or 30 days).
- Skipped visit, holiday, rescheduling and lockout policy (e.g., fee if crew can't get in, with notice time).
- Optional: prepay discount for 3 or 12 months (show total and per-visit equivalent).

## 6. Exclusions (Não incluso)
Things the customer might assume are included: materials, permits, disposal fees, travel outside the service area, paid ads, software licenses, third-party fees, inside oven/fridge or window cleaning, rush delivery.

## 7. Change orders (Alterações de escopo)
Any change in scope is documented in a written change order with price and time impact, approved before the work is done.

## 8. Terms and conditions (Termos e condições)
- Validity: this proposal is valid for 30 days (or the period the user chooses).
- Cancellation: what is owed for work done and materials ordered; deposit refund policy.
- Customer responsibilities: approval deadlines, access, materials.
- Warranty / workmanship (trades): what is covered and for how long, written plainly. A written warranty on consumer products triggers Magnuson-Moss disclosure rules.
- Insurance and license (contractors): general liability carrier and license/registration number (`[fill in]`); workers' comp if required.
- Electronic signature: the proposal can be accepted by e-signature (valid under the federal ESIGN Act and state UETA laws).
- Taxes: the customer pays applicable sales tax; the business handles its own income taxes.
- Contracts with legal, financial or health implications: recommend attorney review.

### When the customer is a consumer
Consumer = an individual buying for personal, family or household use.
- **FTC Cooling-Off Rule**: sale made at the buyer's home ($25 or more) or at a temporary location such as a hotel room, fair or convention ($130 or more). The buyer may cancel within 3 business days; give the oral notice, the written notice in the contract (in the same language as the sales presentation) and two copies of the cancellation form. Refund within 10 days of cancellation. It doesn't cover sales made entirely online, by mail or by phone, but state laws may add rights.
- **Home improvement / contractor work**: many states require a written contract with the contractor's license/registration number, a description of work and materials, start and completion dates, the total price, a payment schedule, a notice of the right to cancel, and caps on the deposit. Examples: Massachusetts Home Improvement Contractor law (registration, deposit limits, 3-day cancellation), New Jersey Contractors' Registration Act, California CSLB rules (small down-payment cap, specific notices), Florida (contracts over $2,500 need the Homeowners' Construction Recovery Fund notice and the lien-law disclosure). Keep the proposal generic and tell the user: "check your state's home improvement rules and license board."
- **Price transparency**: include all mandatory fees in the total (some states, e.g., California, require it). Disclose the refund/cancellation policy.

### Freelancers, contractors and immigration-related services
- W-9: the client will request it; list "Send W-9" in next steps. Payments of $2,000+ in a year (from 2026; $600 before) are reported on Form 1099-NEC. Suggest an EIN instead of sharing an SSN.
- Translation / immigration paperwork: add "We are not attorneys and do not provide legal advice. Only a licensed attorney or a DOJ-accredited representative can advise on immigration matters. We do not guarantee any USCIS decision or processing time." Never call the business a "notario". Certified translations include the translator's signed statement of competence and accuracy.

## 9. Next steps (Próximos passos)
1. Sign the proposal (e-signature or reply "Approved").
2. Pay the deposit / first invoice.
3. Kickoff or first visit within X business days. (B2B freelancer: send W-9.)

## Cover email (English)
> Subject: Proposal — [main outcome] for [Customer]
> Hi [name], attached is the proposal for [main outcome]. In short: [scope in one line], [X] business days, starting at $[amount]. Happy to walk you through it — does [day] at [time] work for a 15-minute call?

## Text message (English)
> Hi [name], it's [your name] from [business]. I just emailed the proposal for [outcome]: [scope], starting at $[amount]. Any questions, just text me here!

## Mensagem (Português)
> Olá, [nome]! Segue a proposta para [resultado principal]: [escopo em 1 linha], prazo de [X] dias úteis, investimento a partir de $[valor]. Podemos conversar [dia] às [hora]?

## Follow-up (3 business days later)
> Hi [name], just checking in — did you get a chance to look at the proposal? If it helps, I can adjust the scope to fit your timing. The pricing holds until [date].

## Final follow-up (validity ending)
> [name], the proposal expires [tomorrow]. Would you like me to hold the [month] slot for you?
